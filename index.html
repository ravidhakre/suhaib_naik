Land · 5 min read

Five things to check before buying land in Goa

Land is the most consequential purchase in a development. It is also the one where buyers most often rely on a brochure, a Google pin and a conversation.

Before I look at the price, I look at five things.

01 — Title and chain of ownership

A clean title is not a formality. Ask for the chain of ownership, survey records, mutation entries and encumbrances. A thirty-year search is a reasonable starting point, and a local lawyer should review what you find.

02 — Zone, conversion and permitted use

The land may be beautiful and still not be buildable for the use you have in mind. Check the zoning, conversion status, setback rules and any restrictions that apply. A plot that cannot be used for your intended purpose is not a bargain at any price.

03 — Access

Legal access is different from physical access. Is there a registered right of way? Is the approach road wide enough for construction vehicles, emergency services and everyday use? Access problems become expensive later.

04 — CRZ, heritage and environmental overlays

Goa has coastal regulation zones, heritage precincts, forest edges and other overlays. These are not minor details. They can determine what can be built, where, and at what cost. Verify before you commit.

05 — Levels, water and what is actually under the ground

Walk the land in the monsoon if you can. Look at slope, drainage, waterlogging, rock, and the cost of getting services to the site. The ground conditions will show up in your construction budget.

The asking price tells you what the land costs today. These checks tell you what it will really take to make it useful. This note is a starting point, not legal advice.

Value · 4 min read

Why two plots in the same village can have very different values

Buyers often compare land by the square metre and the village name. The market is more specific than that.

Two plots in the same village can differ in value because of factors that do not appear in the rate card.

Access and approach

A plot on a narrow, shared approach is not the same as one on a properly registered road. The difference affects construction logistics, privacy and future resale.

Shape, levels and orientation

A rectangular, level plot is easier to plan than an irregular plot with a steep fall. Retaining walls, cut and fill, and drainage all cost money. Orientation affects light, heat and how the house can be arranged.

What surrounds it

A quiet edge, a protected view, a neighbouring commercial use, a future road reservation — these change the experience of living there and the pool of future buyers.

Approvals and clearances

A plot that is ready to build carries a different risk profile from one that still needs conversion, access or environmental clearance. Time and uncertainty have a cost.

The market it actually serves

Is this a local end-user plot, a holiday-home plot, or a development parcel? Each has a different buyer, ticket size and exit. The right comparison is the one that matches the use.

The best way to value land is not a single rate. It is a short list of what can and cannot be done with it.

Construction · 4 min read

What construction taught me about real-estate investment

When you have spent years on site, you stop looking at property the way a brochure presents it.

You start looking at the things that will determine whether the asset performs.

The cost is not the price

The purchase price is the entry ticket. Construction, approvals, services, interiors, landscaping, maintenance and time all sit on top of it. A project can look expensive on paper and still be efficient; another can look cheap and absorb the difference in execution.

Waterproofing is an investment decision

In Goa, the monsoon is not a season you design around later. It is a design condition. Roofs, terraces, windows, drainage and ventilation are not finishing items. They are the difference between a home that ages well and one that keeps asking for money.

Services are part of the product

Electrical load, plumbing, water storage, sewage, lifts, parking and backup are not glamorous. They are also the first things a tenant or buyer notices when they fail.

Maintenance changes the yield

A second home that is empty for months needs someone to open it, check it and repair it. If the asset is rented, the operating model matters as much as the location. Factor in the people and systems that keep it working.

The exit is designed early

The best time to think about resale is before you build. Who will want this property next? What will they compare it with? What will make it easy to say yes? Those questions shape the product, not the other way around.

Construction teaches you to look past the image. That is the lens I bring to an investment conversation.
